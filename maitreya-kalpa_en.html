Maitreya's Descent & The Succession of a Thousand Buddhas

1. Sakyamuni's Prophecy & The Three Assemblies
According to Mahayana scriptures such as the Bodhisattva Womb Sutra, Sakyamuni Buddha, in an assembly long ago, entrusted the great vow of unsurpassed supreme enlightenment to Mahasattva Maitreya. Bodhisattva Maitreya currently resides in the Inner Court of Tusita Heaven as a Bodhisattva bound to one more birth. When conditions ripen, he will descend to the Saha world, attain Buddhahood beneath the towering Dragon Flower Bodhi tree, and hold three great assemblies to deliver those with karmic affinity:

1st Assembly: Delivering 9.6 billion people who received the Five Precepts during Sakyamuni's dharma era.
2nd Assembly: Delivering 9.4 billion people who took the Three Refuges during Sakyamuni's dharma era.
3rd Assembly: Delivering 9.2 billion people who even once recited "Namo Buddha".

This prophecy is perhaps not merely a simple succession of the Dharma lineage, but a compassionate vow of all Buddhas to never abandon sentient beings, continuing life after life.
2. Focus and Repentance for Maitreya's Reception
The Sutra on Maitreya Bodhisattva's Ascent to Tusita Heaven states: "If good men or good women violate precepts and commit various evil karma, upon hearing the compassionate name of this Bodhisattva, if they prostrate themselves fully and sincerely repent, these evil karmas will quickly be purified. For sentient beings in future generations, upon hearing the compassionate name of this Bodhisattva, if they make images of him, offer fragrant flowers, clothing, silk canopies, and banners, and respectfully focus their minds, when such a person's life is about to end, Bodhisattva Maitreya will emit a great ray of light from the white tuft between his eyebrows, and together with heavenly beings, rain down Mandarava flowers to welcome this person. In an instant, this person will be reborn there. Encountering Maitreya, they will bow their heads in reverence, and before even lifting their heads, they will hear the Dharma, attain non-retrogression on the supreme path, and in future lives, encounter Buddhas as numerous as the sands of the Ganges."

The sutra compassionately reveals that even if one has committed transgressions, if they can fully prostrate and sincerely repent, and through making images, making offerings, and maintaining focused reverence, they may hope to evoke the welcoming light from the white tuft. This perhaps inspires us that drawing near to the Inner Court requires concrete practice rather than mere thought.
3. The Trials of the Courts & The Master's Warning
Master Xuanzang of the Tang Dynasty recorded a thought-provoking koan, "The Account of Simhabhadra's Indulgence in the Outer Court," in Volume 5 of The Great Tang Records on the Western Regions:

Bodhisattva Asanga, Bodhisattva Vasubandhu, and their disciple Simhabhadra all vowed to be reborn in the Inner Court of Tusita to see Maitreya, agreeing that whoever passed away first would return to report. Later, Simhabhadra passed away first, but no report came for three years; Vasubandhu also passed away, and no report came for six months. At that time, followers of other schools ridiculed them, assuming they had transmigrated to evil realms.

Later, while Asanga was teaching meditation in the early part of the night, Vasubandhu descended through the void and reported: "After leaving my life here, I was born in a lotus flower among the inner assembly of Maitreya in Tusita Heaven. As the lotus opened, Maitreya praised me, saying, 'Welcome, Broad Wisdom!' Upon completing a circumambulation, I immediately came to report." Asanga asked about Simhabhadra, and Vasubandhu replied: "When I was circumambulating, I saw Simhabhadra among the outer assembly, indulging in sensual pleasures, with no time to look around—how could he come to report?"
4. The Excellence of Both Lands
Exploring the dharma realms of Buddhas, general pure lands are mostly Reward Lands, majestic realms achieved by great Bodhisattvas making unsurpassed vows during their causal stages and cultivating over long eons. The uniqueness of the Inner Court of Tusita Heaven perhaps lies in its foundation within the desire realm of the Saha world, yet transformed into a pure, undefiled realm by the true merit of Bodhisattva Maitreya, who is "bound to one more birth." This perhaps implies that practitioners can directly draw near to the future Buddha without leaving the karmic conditions of the Saha world.

As the Sutra on Maitreya Bodhisattva's Ascent to Tusita Heaven states: "Those who do not loathe birth and death, who find joy in being born in heaven, and who cherish and revere the unsurpassed mind of enlightenment, should contemplate in this manner." This passage perhaps reveals a characteristic of Maitreya's dharma door: it does not prioritize escaping the three realms, but rather facing life and death directly within the desire realm to nurture the mind of enlightenment. The mechanism of non-retrogression in the Pure Land of Ultimate Bliss, on the other hand, perhaps provides another stable foundation for sentient beings. Both lands are excellent places where Buddhas protect and mindfully care for sentient beings; it depends entirely on the practitioner's disposition and which realm resonates with them. According to doctrinal teachings, those born in Ultimate Bliss, embraced by Amitabha's vows, will also achieve ultimate peace, progressing all the way to becoming a Bodhisattva bound to one more birth.
5. The Fortunate Eon & Endless Succession
According to the Bhadrakalpika Sutra, the space-time we reside in is called the Fortunate Eon (Bhadrakalpa). During this lengthy eon, one thousand Buddhas will sequentially descend to the Saha world, perhaps precisely to deliver sentient beings without interruption.

The Dharma is like an eternally unextinguished bright lamp in the dharma realm. Four Buddhas appear in the ninth minor eon, Maitreya descends in the tenth minor eon, and in the fifteenth minor eon, 994 Buddhas will successively manifest, continuing until Rucika Buddha in the twentieth minor eon, seemingly completing the count of the thousand Buddhas.

If practitioners today are embraced by the vow power of the Pure Land, hoping to attain a state of non-retrogression—among them, there may perhaps also be practitioners of great compassion who make the great vows of Samantabhadra, don the armor of immense vows, and return by virtue of their vows. On the path of Bodhi in the future, they will gradually blossom into the pure lotus flowers belonging to our own self-nature.
